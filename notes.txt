Masking: Scales or drops the correctness reward for some correct samples.

What it does: Applies a per-sample multiplier to the correctness component via reward_keep_mask and reward_mask_weight.
Typical effect: If masked and reward_mask_weight=0.5, a correct sample’s correctness reward is halved; if 0.0, it’s dropped. Formatting can optionally be masked if --mask_format is set.
Purpose: Add “discipline” by withholding some positive reinforcement to improve robustness, prevent overfitting, or encourage better exploration while still keeping partial signal.




Zeroing: Sets the entire combined reward to zero, but only for fully-correct samples meeting a format threshold.

What it does: Builds full_keep_mask for fully-correct samples (correctness=1 and format >= format_full_threshold). Selected ones get a 0 multiplier on the total combined reward (correctness_weight * correctness_masked + format_weight * format_effective).
Typical effect: The sample contributes no reward at all when zeroed, regardless of correctness or formatting magnitude.
Purpose: Stronger regularization for “too easy/solved” cases; encourages learning from harder samples or prevents runaway reinforcement on already-mastered behaviors.
Key differences:

Scope: Masking targets the correctness component (and optionally formatting); zeroing targets the full combined reward.
Triggering: Masking applies to correct answers per strategy; zeroing applies only to fully-correct answers gated by formatting threshold (unless you set format_full_threshold=0.0 to ignore format).
Strength: Masking is graded (tunable via reward_mask_weight); zeroing is binary (keep vs zero).
Intent: Masking moderates positive signals; zeroing suppresses signals entirely for selected fully-correct cases.
In your scripts:

Masking is controlled by --reward_mask_strategy, --reward_mask_every_n|prob|cosine|round_robin_k, and --reward_mask_weight; optional --mask_format.
Zeroing is controlled by --full_correct_zero_strategy, its parameters, and --format_full_threshold (or --format_full_threshold 0.0 to ignore format). Warmups: --mask_warmup_steps, --zero_warmup_steps.

GRPO vs DR-GRPO
==============================================================================

You’re right — everything we’ve run so far used DR‑GRPO (the default), not “pure” GRPO. Here’s the crisp diff and what it means in our setup.

Objective shape

GRPO: vanilla policy‑gradient on completion tokens with advantage normalization.
Loss (per sequence): L = − E[ A · Σ_t log πθ(a_t|s_t) ].
In code: implemented via the “stop‑grad trick” r = exp(logπ − sg(logπ)) so the forward value is 1 but gradients match REINFORCE.
DR‑GRPO: clipped/regularized variant of GRPO for stability.
Core idea: limit excessively large per‑token updates by clipping the update factor within [1−ε, 1+ε], optionally add KL to a reference model.
Typical form: L = − E[ A · clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε) ] + β·KL(πθ || πref). In our runs β=0 (so no KL), but clipping is active.
What we actually used

Loss type: dr_grpo (default in the runner and main script).
Epsilon: epsilon_low=0.2 (and epsilon_high=None → symmetric clip at ±0.2).
KL: beta=0.0 → no ref‑KL; stability comes from clipping only.
Differences across experiments so far were from reward masking/zeroing and schedules, not from changing the loss family.
Why it matters

GRPO (no clipping): can move faster (bigger updates) but is more volatile; sometimes better for tasks needing large policy shifts.
DR‑GRPO (clipped): typically more stable and less likely to overreact to noisy rewards; can slightly slow progress or dampen exploration.
How to switch to “pure GRPO”

Keep everything else identical and set --loss_type grpo (you can still use Liger for speed).

===================================================================================================
PHASE SPLIT Masking
===================================================================================================

Intuitive

We train in two phases: “consolidation” then “planning.” While training, we watch how uncertain the model’s completions are. Uncertainty is measured by the average per-token negative log-likelihood (think: how surprised the model is by its own outputs).

Simple Rule
After an initial warmup, we compute a sliding-window average of that uncertainty. When it stays below a chosen target for several consecutive windows (patience), we declare consolidation “done” and switch to planning.
Why That Matters

Below-target uncertainty means the model is reliably producing well-formed, low-entropy answers on the basics; switching phase lets us alter training emphasis (e.g., adjust length/chain budget and add a small diversity incentive) to push broader, harder reasoning.
Key knobs (plain English)

Target: how low the average uncertainty must be.
Window: how many steps we average over.
Patience: how many windows in a row must be below target.
Warmup: don’t check until early training noise settles.

Acknowledged. Plan: give a concise, plain-English summary of the masking used in this run, with the key knobs and specific values.

Masking approach: phase-split masking (format-aware reward masking on a cadence).
Cadence: masks some rewards on every Nth correct sample (N=20) to prevent overfitting to easy patterns and encourage broader coverage.
Soft mask: uses a weight (0.7) so masked rewards aren’t hard-zeroed; they’re down-weighted rather than removed.
Format-aware: applies the mask to format rewards as well (mask_format=True), keeping the model aligned to the required <answer>…</answer> output.
Warmup: masking only starts after an initial stabilization (mask_warmup_steps=400).
Full-correct zeroing: not used in this specific phase-split run (full_correct_zero_strategy=none). For contrast, the best continuous run uses full-correct zero every 20 after a 200-step warmup.

In the 10-task sweep cont was better but on OOD split is better

Observation: Continuous (full-zero-every-20) led on the 10-task in-sweep eval, while phase-split masking edged it out on the 30-task OOD set.
Likely cause — generalization vs specialization:
Format-aware masking: Phase-split down-weights rewards on a cadence and applies masking to format, which tends to harden adherence to the required output shape; this pays off on OOD tasks that penalize format slips.
Phase switch: Once uncertainty drops, training shifts emphasis (chain/length budget and a small diversity bonus), encouraging broader reasoning patterns rather than overfitting to the in-sweep distribution.
Diversity bonus: Small distinct-2 nudges variety post-switch, helping unfamiliar structures in stretch tasks.
Task mix: On Core-like tasks, both are close; on Stretch, phase-split typically shows small but consistent gains, which accumulates to a higher overall.
Continuous full-zero: Strong at tightening correctness on seen patterns, but can over-prune “easy wins” reinforcement without boosting flexibility needed for OOD shifts.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
masking vs. zeroing
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Correctness masking: always applies after warmup; correctness reward is multiplied by the mask from the chosen strategy (every_n, prob_p, etc.) with soft weight via reward_mask_weight.
Format masking: optional; controlled by mask_format. When mask_format=True, the same mask is applied to the format reward; when False, format reward is left unmasked.
Warmups: mask_warmup_steps delays any masking until training stabilizes.
Full-correct zeroing: separate mechanism (full_correct_zero_strategy, full_correct_zero_every_n/prob/k/cosine) that zeros rewards for fully correct and well-formatted completions after zero_warmup_steps; independent of the down-weighting mask.
Overrides: per-problem overrides can toggle mask_format and adjust format_weight for specific task types.
